Pilot exploring AI-enabled execution in venture education

AI can compress execution time. This pilot explores what students can build—and what they learn—when managed well.

AI tools can accelerate research, modeling, and prototyping. The constraint becomes management skill: defining objectives, delegating work, evaluating output quality, and iterating toward better decisions.

View pilot design See the student journey

Why this matters

AI can dramatically increase the pace of execution. The differentiator is no longer simply speed—it is judgment: the ability to scope work, set constraints, and recognize when outputs are off.

  • More iteration: teams can test and refine ideas more times within the same timebox.
  • Stronger evaluation: students must identify weak assumptions, missing evidence, and flawed logic.
  • Better delegation: clear deliverables and success criteria become essential management practice.

At a glance

  • Format: pilot (options: short intensive, course module, or workshop)
  • Primary skill: delegation + evaluation (management fundamentals)
  • Student outputs: venture concept, analysis, prototype/service model, pitch narrative
  • Platform: Nebula One (governed access, cost controls, compliance)

Fit with Ivey

Ivey's experiential, case-based approach builds the management judgment students need to use AI effectively. AI does not replace fundamentals—it accelerates execution so students can apply those fundamentals through more cycles of work.

First pilot context: New Venture Projects (subject to faculty interest and fit).

Why now

With Nebula One launching, this is a contained, practical testbed to explore student and faculty workflows and generate real feedback on academic AI usage.

  • Clear scope and bounded duration
  • Actionable insights for teaching and platform adoption
  • Low-risk experimentation with meaningful outputs

Next step

Confirm feasibility with faculty partners and identify a pilot format that fits within existing programming. The goal at this stage is to define a small, high-impact pilot and agree on success measures.
